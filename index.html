The anchor series · working name

LARPing a CEO

Running an agency from my bedroom until it's real — watching the 5-to-9 catch the 9-to-5.

What it is

The one series worth committing to, because it's the only thing on your whole slate nobody else can make. You're building agency income while starting a pharmacy career — two income streams, one safe, one a bet, pulling against each other. The series follows the side hustle trying to overtake the salary. It films at home, at your desk, on your phone, at any hour you get in — which is exactly why it survives your schedule when a location-dependent series wouldn't.

The goal in the background

The 5-to-9 overtakes the 9-to-5. Revenue is how you keep score; the day-job-versus-side-hustle tension is the story. It barely shows in most videos — it's the occasional check-in that makes the whole thing feel like it's climbing somewhere, so people follow instead of just watching.

The tone (this is the whole trick)

Never corporate. Open cold on whatever you're actually feeling in that moment — the real hook — then drop the silly self-aware bump: "…anyway, welcome back to LARPing a CEO." Then the genuinely useful content. Unserious wrapper, serious substance. The daftness is what makes the money and business talk go down easy; it signals you're not a finance guy performing success, you're a 22-year-old larping a company into existence until it's true.

Every idea

The Five Shots

Capture these every day, whatever the day is. You're not filming an episode — you're filming raw material. The edit gets decided later.

Why this is the actual system

The reason creators run dry isn't a shortage of ideas — it's that the good day already happened and nobody filmed it. Five shots takes ninety seconds across a day. Do it for a fortnight and you'll have more footage than you can cut.

Film first, judge later. The clip you think is pointless is often the one that carries the video. Never delete on the day.

The gap now: they watch, they don't follow

That's what the anchor series is for — a reason to follow rather than just watch — and why your reach videos should end on something that makes a stranger want the next one.
