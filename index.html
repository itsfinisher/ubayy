The Week

One episode of each series per week, slotted against your actual job. Five anchors, two flex days.

The rule that makes it survivable

Anchors are non-negotiable; everything else is optional. If a week collapses, you post five things and nothing goes dark. If a week is good, the flex days catch the overflow.

Sunday is a shoot day, not a posting day. Batch three to four Notes episodes in one sitting — same jumper, different angles, so they don't read as one session. That's your buffer for the weeks when work eats everything.

Reading the week for balance

Side Quest is your only Reach anchor — the one series built to be found by people who've never heard of you. The Agency, Twenties Notes and Starting From Zero build Trust; Grown Up Things builds Loyalty.

But every series now has Reach, Trust and Loyalty episodes in the bank, so the day doesn't dictate the job. On a week where growth has stalled, you can pull a Reach episode out of any of the five — Twenties Notes has rankings, Grown Up Things has cost breakdowns, Starting From Zero has beginner lists. Side Quest is still the heaviest Reach series and probably always will be, but it's no longer carrying it alone.

The Five Shots

Capture these every day, whatever the day is. You're not filming an episode — you're filming raw material. The episode gets decided later.

Why this is the actual system

The reason creators run dry isn't a shortage of ideas — it's that the good day already happened and nobody filmed it. You can't decide something is content after the fact.

Five shots takes about ninety seconds spread across a day. Do it for a fortnight and you'll have more usable footage than you can cut. The point isn't that every clip gets used — it's that the interesting day is never the one you missed.

The rule inside the rule

Film first, judge later. The clip you think is pointless while you're filming it is very often the one that carries the video. You are the worst possible judge of your own footage in the moment you're standing in it.

Corollary: never delete on the day.

Where the shots go

Everything you capture lands in one of the five series — you're rarely filming for a specific episode. A meeting that went badly is Agency b-roll and a Notes idea. A café you liked is Side Quest. The walk home after a long shift is Grown Up Things. Same afternoon, three series.

That's the whole trick to posting five times a week on a full-time job: you're not producing five things, you're capturing continuously and cutting five things out of it.

The three stages, plainly

Every video does one of three jobs. Not better or worse jobs — different ones. The tags on each series and each episode tell you which.

Reach

Gets found by strangers. Needs no context. Someone who has never heard of you can watch it and get the whole thing: a ranking, a price, a place, a number. This is how new people arrive.

Trust

Turns a viewer into a follower. They have seen you once and this is the one that makes them tap follow — an opinion, a mistake, something honest. It needs a little context to land, which is why it will not reach cold.

Loyalty

Keeps the people you already have. Barely travels to strangers and is not meant to. It is for the people already invested — the arc, the recap, the ongoing story. This is what makes a page feel like a show.

You need all three. A page that is only Reach gets views and no followers. A page that is only Loyalty is what you have now.

Where your bank currently sits

Every series now carries all three, so you are never stuck with the wrong job on the wrong day. If the follower count is flat, filter the bank to Reach whatever series is up. If people watch once and never return, filter to Loyalty.

What this actually changes on a Monday

Nothing about your five series. You still post one of each a week. The difference is that when you open the bank, you can now ask a better question than "what do I fancy filming."

If your last three videos were all Loyalty — a recap, an arc episode, a personal one — the next one should be Reach, or you are only ever talking to the people already there. That is the whole idea. It is a balance check, not a rule.

The diagnosis

Those two numbers together tell a specific story. The people who already follow you watch, like and comment at a rate most creators would take. But 0.24× views per follower means the algorithm isn't carrying you past them — your videos are landing with your existing audience and stopping there.

That's not an ideas problem. It's partly a first-three-seconds problem — you're being served to new people and they leave before the video works. But it's also a mix problem: most of what you make is Trust and Loyalty content, which is exactly the content that doesn't travel to strangers by design.

What to fix first

  • Open mid-scene. No "hey guys," no setup. Start on the interesting bit and explain backwards.
  • Say the stake out loud. "A brand offered us four grand and I turned it down" beats "come to work with me."
  • Put the episode number in the caption, not the first frame. A stranger who sees "Episode 12" before they know what the show is has been given homework.
  • Track hook type, not just views. After twenty videos you'll know whether questions, numbers or cold-opens travel for you. Nobody can tell you that in advance.

On being the niche

You said you want people to like you, not the content. That's the right target and the wrong order. Nobody attaches to a personality in the abstract — they attach to someone doing something, and the personality is why they stay.

So the five series are the vehicle. The bit where you and Rayyan are arguing about somewhere to eat on the way to a meeting is not the offcut between the real content. It's the reason anyone comes back. Keep it in.

Why these five last

None of them are tied to a job or a city. Leave pharmacy and Grown Up Things still works — it was never about pharmacy. Move abroad and Side Quest just gets a new map. Hire staff and The Agency gets better. Get fit and Starting From Zero becomes the story of how you did it, told by someone who remembers being unable to.

The one to bet on is Side Quest. The through-line in everything you've described isn't travel or business — it's that you keep saying yes to things.

The app, when you're ready

It's off the slate for now. When you want it, it doesn't need its own series — the build lives inside The Agency as a storyline, and the launch becomes a run of episodes rather than a permanent slot. That way you're not committed to weekly app content before you've decided to be public about it.
