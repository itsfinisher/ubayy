Your Voice

The craft rules that are specifically yours — drawn from what already works on your page, not generic advice. This is the part that's hard to copy.

Voiceover is your engine — for nearly everything

You can make almost all of it voiceover over b-roll, and you should. Even the "talking-head" ideas: film yourself saying it if you want the take, then strip the audio and lay it over footage. That collapses your whole workflow to one mode — capture footage, capture voice, marry them later — and the two halves can happen on different days in different states. For your schedule that's the whole game.

The one exception worth keeping: every sixth or seventh video, actually show your face for the key moment — the confession, the big opinion, a comment reply looking straight down the lens. Not because voiceover is worse, but because "the mate" needs to be seen occasionally or the polish starts feeling like glass. Voiceover for reach and volume; the occasional real face so it stays a person, not a channel.

The short version: voiceover by default, face on purpose.

The first five seconds

Your best videos are won here and you already know the recipe: heat flash, shutter or click sound, motion, and the feeling you're in stated fast. This isn't a style choice to vary — it's the thing that's working, so it's fixed. If a video doesn't have a cold open that stops a thumb, it isn't finished.

The "feeling hook" and the "heat flash" are the same move: what you say is the feeling, how it's cut is the flash. They land together.

Value = entertainment OR information OR your take

Your own test, and it's a good one. Every video should clearly be at least one of the three. If it's none, it doesn't go up. Most of your strongest ideas are two at once — a deal breakdown that's informative AND has your opinion in it; a trip that entertains AND shows a real place.

Leave the door open (this is your biggest lever)

Your comments are quiet, and for "the mate who figured some stuff out," that's the actual bottleneck — the whole identity is a back-and-forth, and right now it's a monologue. The fix is mechanical, not charisma:

  • End on something answerable. Not "follow for more." A question, a mild wrong opinion people want to correct, an unfinished thought, a "tell me I'm not the only one."
  • Reply properly, early. The first hour of replies teaches people the comments are a place you actually are.
  • Film replies as videos. A good comment answered on camera is the single clearest "this is a two-way thing" signal there is — and it's free content that needs no idea.
  • Pin the one that starts a conversation, not the one that praises you.

You said you'll work them. Then this is probably worth more than any single video idea in the pool — it's what turns the views you're already getting into the follows you're not.
